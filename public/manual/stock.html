Stock verification

Stock verification compares scanned barcodes with a fixed collection snapshot. One open session is allowed per library.

How to use this section

  1. Open Stock verification and create a named session for the selected library.
  2. Scan copies against the session. Repeated scans do not add duplicate expected records.
  3. Review seen and unseen items, especially copies that were already on loan at session start.
  4. Investigate discrepancies before closing. Closure reports discrepancies without automatically changing copy statuses.
  5. Export findings for approval. Correct later copy status through Catalogue with a reason.

Notes and limits

The expected inventory is fixed at session creation; reconcile later acquisitions and circulation separately.

Do not classify every unscanned item as permanently lost without physical and circulation checks.

Worked example: annual stock check

RBCP has 1,000 non-withdrawn copies when the session starts. Of these, 80 are already issued and 920 should normally be on the shelves. The librarian scans 900 shelf copies. The report therefore distinguishes 900 seen copies, 80 copies on loan at the snapshot, and 20 unscanned non-loaned copies requiring investigation.

  1. Search the shelves, return trolley, repair area and circulation records for the 20 unscanned copies. An unscanned copy may be misplaced or awaiting shelving.
  2. Do not mark the 80 issued copies as missing simply because they are not physically present. They were expected to be with borrowers.
  3. If 10 newly purchased copies are added after the session starts, reconcile them separately; the original snapshot stays fixed.
  4. Export the findings and obtain the required institutional decision. Closing the session does not automatically mark any copy lost or missing. Correct a copy status in Catalogue only after investigation and record a reason.

Stock verification checks the physical collection against the register. It is different from a circulation report, which records borrowing activity. Scan a barcode or accession for each physical copy; two copies of the same title are separate inventory items.
